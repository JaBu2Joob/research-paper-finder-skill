# Academic Sources for Research Paper Search

This document lists reputable academic databases and search engines that can be utilized for comprehensive research paper discovery. Each source may have specific search parameters or API access methods that should be considered during implementation.

## Primary Sources:

*   **Google Scholar**: A widely used, free search engine that indexes scholarly literature across an array of publishing formats and disciplines. It provides access to articles, theses, books, abstracts, and court opinions from academic publishers, professional societies, online repositories, universities, and other web sites.
*   **PubMed**: A free resource supporting the search and retrieval of biomedical and life sciences literature. It is maintained by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) at the U.S. National Library of Medicine (NLM), located at the National Institutes of Health (NIH).
*   **arXiv**: An open-access archive for scholarly articles in physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and systems science, and economics. Papers are not peer-reviewed before posting.
*   **Semantic Scholar**: An AI-powered research tool for scientific literature, developed at the Allen Institute for AI. It uses machine learning to provide summaries, extract key information, and identify connections between papers.
*   **IEEE Xplore Digital Library**: A powerful resource for discovery and access to scientific and technical content published by the IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) and its publishing partners. It covers publications in electrical engineering, computer science, and electronics.
*   **Scopus**: A large abstract and citation database of peer-reviewed literature: scientific journals, books, and conference proceedings. Delivering a comprehensive overview of the world's research output in the fields of science, technology, medicine, social sciences, and arts and humanities.

## Considerations for Implementation:

*   **API Access**: Investigate available APIs for programmatic access to search functionality and metadata extraction for each source.
*   **Search Syntax**: Understand the specific query syntax and filtering options supported by each platform.
*   **Rate Limits**: Be aware of any rate limits or usage policies for automated queries.
*   **Data Format**: Anticipate varying data formats (e.g., JSON, XML) for search results and citations.
*   **Authentication**: Determine if API keys or other authentication methods are required for access.
